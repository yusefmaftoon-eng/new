What kachence/polymm's strategy would earn on a market that isn't real

200 independent runs of polymm's actual de-vig math against a documented, parameterized synthetic model of bookmaker odds and Polymarket order books — because this sandbox can't reach Polymarket, the-odds-api, or any real market data.

Strategy source: github.com/kachence/polymm  ·  de-vig math reused verbatim from src/core/vig_removal.py

Why synthetic, not real

This environment's network policy blocks data-api.polymarket.com, gamma-api.polymarket.com, and the-odds-api.com outright (confirmed by hand — each rejects the connection at the proxy). polymm also isn't a backtester: it's a live orchestrator with two websockets and no bundled historical dataset. So neither a real backtest nor a real paper trade is possible from here. What follows instead runs the bot's actual pricing logic against a synthetic, fully-documented market model — every assumption is a parameter you can see, question, and change in polymm_sim/simulate.py. Treat every number below as illustrative of the strategy's mechanics, not a forecast.

The strategy

Arb captured by market-making, with a residual left over

Pull bookmaker odds from several books, strip the vig to get a fair win probability. If that fair value sits at least 7 percentage points above Polymarket's best bid — polymm's real min_edge default — quote a limit buy one cent above the bid, sized at 10 shares (also the real default). If it fills, immediately try to buy the other outcome too, so both legs together cost under $1/share: a locked arbitrage. When that second leg doesn't fill in time, the position is left directional — a bet that only pays off if the original team actually wins.

How much does one assumption matter

Turning off adverse selection

The residual leg's negative average isn't discovered by this simulation — it's injected, deliberately, to model the real writeup's finding that unhedged positions are disproportionately the ones the bot's own fair-value estimate got wrong (see adverse_selection_edge_erosion in the code). Run it yourself

Reproduce or change any of this

# single run, human-readable summary
python3 polymm_sim/simulate.py --n-matches 3000 --seed 42

# the full 200-seed report behind this page
python3 polymm_sim/run_report.py --n-seeds 200 --n-matches 1500 --out report.json

# try a faster bot (lower latency = less adverse selection)
python3 polymm_sim/simulate.py --hedge-latency-sd 0.3 --n-matches 3000

# the naive case with no adverse selection modeled
python3 polymm_sim/simulate.py --adverse-selection-edge-erosion 0 --n-matches 3000
